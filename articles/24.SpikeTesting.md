# Spike Testing

A **spike test** verifies whether your system survives—and recovers cleanly from—a **sudden, extreme surge of traffic** over a very short window. Common real-world triggers include concert ticket drops, flash sales, push-notification blasts, broadcast commercials, and tax or filing deadlines.

Unlike a [Stress Test](23.StressTesting.md) that ramps up gradually over minutes so caches and autoscalers can adapt, a spike test jumps from baseline to extreme concurrency in **seconds** with zero warning.

---

## What a Spike Test Answers

- **Shock absorption:** Do connection queues, thread pools, API gateways, and rate limiters absorb an instant burst without crashing (`502`/`503`/OOM)?
- **Autoscaler lag:** How does the current footprint behave **before** horizontal autoscalers finish spinning up new instances (which often takes 30–120 seconds)?
- **Immediate recovery:** Once the flash crowd leaves, do response times and error rates snap straight back to normal baseline levels, or stay stuck in retry storms and exhausted pools?

---

## Runnable YAML Spike Test Plan

To model a clean **Baseline → Sudden Surge → Recovery** curve in LPS without dropping traffic to zero, combine three additive `stages` in one round with `startupDelay` timed against the iteration's per-client `duration`.

**Example assumptions:** normal baseline is 20 clients, a flash surge injects **+150 clients in ~3 seconds** (`arrivalDelay: 20` ms), and every client runs for 60 seconds (`duration: 60`) from its own start time.

```yaml
name: SuddenSpikeTest
variables:
- name: targetUrl
  value: https://api.example.com/resource
rounds:
- name: SpikeAndRecoveryRound
  stages:
  - numberOfClients: 20
    arrivalDelay: 250
  - numberOfClients: 150
    arrivalDelay: 20
    startupDelay: 50000
  - numberOfClients: 20
    arrivalDelay: 250
    startupDelay: 52000
  iterations:
  - name: SpikeResource
    mode: D
    duration: 60
    httpRequest:
      url: $targetUrl
      httpMethod: GET
    failureRules:
    - metric: "ErrorRate > 0.05"
      errorStatusCodes: ">= 500"
    - metric: "TotalTime.P95 > 2000"
    terminationRules:
    - metric: "ErrorRate > 0.25"
      errorStatusCodes: ">= 500"
      gracePeriod: "00:00:15"
    - metric: "TotalTime.P95 > 5000"
      gracePeriod: "00:00:15"
```

Run the plan with:

```bash
lps run SuddenSpikeTest.yaml
```

---

## How This Traffic Behaves (`~2m 55s` Total)

1. **Steady baseline (`0:05–0:55`):** Stage 1 spawns 20 clients over ~4.8 seconds, then `startupDelay: 50000` holds a clean **20-client** baseline for 50 seconds so you can see normal latency.
2. **Vertical 3-second surge (`0:55–1:55`):** Stage 2 injects 150 additional clients 20 ms apart (~3 seconds). Active concurrency jumps sharply to **170** (`20 + 150`) before Stage 1 finishes around `1:00–1:05`, holding **150 active spike clients** for ~1 minute.
3. **Sharp drop to recovery (`1:55–2:50`):** Stage 3 starts around `1:50` (`startupDelay: 52000`) so 20 fresh recovery clients warm up right as the 150 spike clients reach their 60-second `duration` between `1:55` and `1:58`—leaving a steady **20-client** recovery plateau for ~52 seconds.

> **How LPS stages work:** Stages in LPS are additive spawns with natural per-client completion—LPS never force-kills active clients mid-request. Timing Stage 3 right as Stage 2's 60-second `duration` expires produces the sharp drop back to 20 active clients cleanly.

## Best Practices

- **Tailor the endpoint to the real spike event:** Flash crowds usually hammer one or two hot paths (such as `/login`, `/DropItem`, or `/checkout`) rather than browsing the whole application evenly.
- **Avoid "rollercoaster" multi-spike scripts first:** Start with a single clean spike + recovery window so bottlenecks and recovery time are easy to isolate.
- **Check generator socket limits:** Spawning 150+ clients in 3 seconds opens sockets rapidly—verify `lps httpclient --maxconnectionsperserver` and `lps watchdog` on the load generator beforehand.
